# FARO ESP32 work log

## 2026-09-17 — Sonar, LED, and passive-buzzer bring-up

### Objective

Establish a minimal on-device distance-feedback proof of concept using the
CENFOTEC IdeaBoard / ESP32 and HC-SR04-style ultrasonic module reused from
MiSumoBot.

### Verified hardware configuration

- **Board:** CENFOTEC IdeaBoard with ESP32-WROOM-32E
- **Sonar power requirement:** IdeaBoard `SELECT` ↔ `Vin` jumper installed and
  external battery pack connected so the sonar receives 5 V.
- **Sonar signal pins:**
  - `TRIG` → GPIO 25
  - `ECHO` → GPIO 26
- **Onboard LED:** WS2812B / NeoPixel on GPIO 2
- **Passive buzzer:** `+` → GPIO 27; `−` → GND

The HC-SR04 ECHO output is nominally 5 V. It must be level-shifted or divided
before reaching the ESP32's 3.3 V GPIO 26 unless the particular intervening
IdeaBoard/shield connection already provides that protection.

### Pin-mapping finding

The CENFOTEC materials conflict on the ultrasonic pin direction:

- `sumobot/sumobot.ino` documents `TRIG=26`, `ECHO=25`.
- The dedicated `PruebaUltrasonico/PruebaUltrasonico.ino`, the physical
  connection diagram, and the 2024 connection documentation use
  `TRIG=25`, `ECHO=26`.

The first FARO sketch used `TRIG=26`, `ECHO=25` and consistently produced
`distance: no echo` with a purple LED. Switching FARO to `TRIG=25`,
`ECHO=26` resolved the issue on the actual hardware. Treat this latter mapping
as the verified FARO wiring contract.

### Buzzer identification

Two visually similar two-pin buzzers were tested separately.

- The unit producing a continuous tone from direct 5 V DC was identified as an
  **active buzzer**. It is not used for variable-distance tones.
- The other unit was selected as the **passive buzzer** and responds to PWM
  generated by the ESP32 on GPIO 27.

### Historical bench behavior before BLE mode gating

`esp32.ino` samples sonar distance, uses a rolling median of five valid
readings, and presents the result through both the onboard LED and passive
buzzer:

- no echo or distance at/over 2 m: purple LED for no echo; buzzer silent;
- 1–2 m: blue LED; slow 1.2 kHz pulses;
- 0.5–1 m: amber LED; faster 1.6 kHz pulses;
- under 0.5 m: red LED; urgent 2.2 kHz pulses.

This records the initial hardware bring-up. In the current firmware, the LED
and telemetry may continue reporting those bands while **Inactive**, but the
passive buzzer emits the listed tones only while **Navigating**.

### Hardware validation

Validated on the physical hardware:

- sonar returns distance measurements;
- WS2812B color bands track the distance;
- passive-buzzer pitch/cadence tracks the same distance bands.

### BLE operating-mode validation — 2026-09-17

The ESP32 BLE firmware was compiled with Arduino CLI and flashed to the physical
IdeaBoard on `/dev/ttyUSB0`. Flash verification completed successfully and the
serial monitor continued to report filtered sonar distances after reset.

Physical iPhone validation confirmed:

- the phone discovered and connected to `FARO-Obstacle`;
- the module started in **Inactive** mode with its local buzzer disarmed;
- switching to **Navigating** from the phone armed the local distance-feedback
  behavior;
- switching back to **Inactive** stopped it.

The immediate local sonar-to-buzzer path remains owned by the ESP32 while
Navigating. Disconnect-retention and reset-to-Inactive should remain explicit
regression checks whenever the BLE transport or power behavior changes.

### BLE startup assertion regression — 2026-09-17

The first BLE flash asserted in `BLECharacteristic::notify()` because firmware
attempted to notify the initial mode before `BLEService::start()` attached the
characteristic to a server. The initial Inactive byte is now set without a
notification before service startup; notifications begin only after the service
is live.

The corrected image was compiled and flashed to the same IdeaBoard. A serial
monitor remained running and emitted sonar samples without the assertion or a
reset loop.

### Printable holder asset — 2026-09-18

`FARO-holder.stl` was added at the repository root as the printable prototype
holder model. STL files do not encode units, and no print, fit, or wearing
acceptance is recorded yet; verify scale and physical fit before use.
