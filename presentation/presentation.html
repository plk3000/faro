FARO

Familiar-space Awareness, Recognition, and Orientation

Remembering places. Recognizing hazards. Co-designed with lived experience.

Native iPhone app + private vision service + ESP32 module

FARO means lighthouse in Spanish. Frame the project as a personalized secondary companion, never as a replacement for established mobility aids.

Familiar spaces can still be disorienting

The personal question

“Where am I in my own environment?”

Generic object recognition cannot answer this by itself.

Orientation

Entering the wrong room or losing track of location.

Context

Understanding nearby objects without a visually demanding interface.

Obstacles

Noticing furniture or another object in the forward path.

FARO is a secondary assistive companion—not a replacement for a white cane, guide dog, or mobility training.

The motivation is personal and familiar-space specific: orientation, requested visual context, and immediate forward-obstacle awareness.

One companion, three distinct jobs

Personal memory

Remember

Learn user-named places from several camera views, then recognize them on-device—or report uncertainty.

Requested context

Describe

Send one user-requested still to a private vision service and speak a concise bilingual description.

Local reflex

Warn

Measure forward distance on the ESP32 and drive passive-buzzer tones without waiting for cloud AI.

  Large controls, VoiceOver, English and Spanish, touch and voice access

Keep the three jobs distinct. The cloud narration path is advisory; the obstacle tone path is local and independent.

Clear boundaries are part of the design

One forward sonar

Can miss thin, soft, angled, high or low obstacles, stairs, and drop-offs.

Advisory AI

Scene narration can be wrong or unavailable and never controls the local warning.

Coarse location

GPS may filter different sites; it is never presented as room-level evidence.

Foreground wake

Siri or another supported action must first bring FARO to the foreground.

Recognition uncertainty

Large viewpoint or lighting changes can reduce confidence; FARO must not force a guess.

Prototype hardware

Distance bands are starting points, and the printable holder still needs fit validation.

Assistive prototype · secondary companion · not clinically validated or production-ready

Responsible framing is a product feature. Avoid claims that one sensor covers every hazard or that the prototype replaces established mobility tools.
